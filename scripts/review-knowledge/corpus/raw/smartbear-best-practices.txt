Highly regimented peer reviews can stifle productivity, yet lackadaisical processes are often ineffective. Managers are responsible for finding a middle ground where peer review can be efficient and effective while fostering open communication and knowledge-share between teammates.</p> <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-navy-600 mt-12 mb-4">10 tips for effective peer code review</h2> <h3 id="review-fewer-than-400-lines-of-code-at-a-time" class="text-xl font-semibold text-navy-600 mt-8 mb-3">1. Review fewer than 400 lines of code at a time</h3> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">A <a href="https://smartbear.com/resources/case-studies/cisco-systems-collaborator/">SmartBear study of a Cisco Systems programming team</a> revealed that developers should review no more than 200 to 400 lines of code (LOC) at a time. The brain can only effectively process so much information at a time; beyond 400 LOC, the ability to find defects diminishes.</p> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">In practice, a review of 200-400 LOC over 60 to 90 minutes should yield 70-90% defect discovery. So, if 10 defects existed in the code, a properly conducted review would find between seven and nine of them.</p> <h3 id="take-your-time-inspection-rates-should-under-500-loc-per-hour" class="text-xl font-semibold text-navy-600 mt-8 mb-3">2. Take your time. Inspection rates should under 500 LOC per hour</h3> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">It can be tempting to tear through a review, assuming that someone else will catch the errors that you don&apos;t find. However, SmartBear research shows a significant drop in defect density at rates faster than 500 LOC per hour. Code reviews in reasonable quantity, at a slower pace for a limited amount of time results in the most effective code review.</p> <h3 id="do-not-review-for-more-than-60-minutes-at-a-time" class="text-xl font-semibold text-navy-600 mt-8 mb-3">3. Do not review for more than 60 minutes at a time</h3> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">Just as you shouldn&apos;t review code too quickly, you also should not review for too long in one sitting. When people engage in any activity requiring concentrated effort over a period of time, performance starts dropping off after about 60 minutes. Studies show that taking breaks from a task over a period of time can greatly improve quality of work. Conducting more frequent reviews should reduce the need to ever have to conduct a review of this length.</p> <h3 id="set-goals-and-capture-metrics" class="text-xl font-semibold text-navy-600 mt-8 mb-3">4. Set goals and capture metrics</h3> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">Before implementing a process, your team should decide how you will measure the effectiveness of peer review and name a few tangible goals.</p> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">Using <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMART_criteria" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">SMART criteria</a>, start with external metrics. For example, &quot;reduce support calls by 15%,&quot; or &quot;cut the percentage of defects injected by development in half.&quot; This information should give you a quantifiable picture of how your code is improving. &quot;Fix more bugs&quot; is not an effective goal.</p> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">It&apos;s also useful to watch internal process metrics, including:</p> <ul class="mb-5 pl-6 space-y-2 text-dark"> <li class="text-dark"><strong class="font-semibold">Inspection rate:</strong> the speed with which a review is performed</li> <li class="text-dark"><strong class="font-semibold">Defect rate:</strong> the number of bugs found per hour of review</li> <li class="text-dark"><strong class="font-semibold">Defect density:</strong> the average number of bugs found per line of code</li> </ul> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">Realistically, only automated or strictly controlled processes can provide repeatable metrics. A metrics-driven code review tool gathers data automatically so that your information is accurate and without human bias. To get a better sense of effective code review reporting, you can see how our code review tool, <a href="/product/collaborator/" class="text-blue-500 hover:text-blue-800">Collaborator</a>, does it.</p> <h3 id="authors-should-annotate-source-code-before-the-review" class="text-xl font-semibold text-navy-600 mt-8 mb-3">5. Authors should annotate source code before the review</h3> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">Authors should annotate code before the review occurs because annotations guide the reviewer through the changes, showing which files to look at first and defending the reason behind each code modification. Annotations should be directed at other reviewers to ease the process and provide more depth in context. As an added benefit, the author will often find additional errors before the peer review even begins. More bugs found prior to peer review will yield in lower defect density because fewer bugs exist overall.</p> <h3 id="use-checklists" class="text-xl font-semibold text-navy-600 mt-8 mb-3">6. Use checklists</h3> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">It&apos;s very likely that each person on your team makes the same 10 mistakes over and over. Omissions in particular are the hardest defects to find because it&apos;s difficult to review something that isn&apos;t there. Checklists are the most effective way to eliminate frequently made errors and to combat the challenges of omission finding. Code review checklists also provide team members with clear expectations for each type of review and can be helpful to track for reporting and process improvement purposes.</p> <!-- Inline CTA --> <div class="relative p-12 bg-indigo-500 overflow-hidden cubes-background-navy-monotone"> <div class="flex flex-col lg:flex-row items-center justify-between gap-6 relative"> <div> <h3 class="text-xxl font-bold text-neutral-900 mb-4">Peer Review Checklists</h3> <p class="body text-sm leading-1.55 text-neutral-900 font-semibold">Understand the code review process</p> </div> <div class="shrink-0"> <a class="button inline-flex items-center gap-2" href="https://smartbear.com/learn/code-review/guide-to-code-review-process/">

Establish a process for fixing defects found</h3> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">Even after optimizing code review processes by time-boxing reviews, limiting LOC reviewed per hour and naming key metrics for your team, there&apos;s still a key review step missing. How will the bugs be fixed? It seems obvious, but many teams do not have a systematic method for <em>fixing</em> the bugs they&apos;ve worked so hard to find.</p> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">The best way to ensure that defects are fixed is to use a collaborative code review tool that allows reviewers to log bugs, discuss them with the author, and approve changes in the code. Without an automated tool, bugs found in review likely aren&apos;t logged in the team&apos;s usual defect tracking system because they are found before code is released to QA.</p> <h3 id="foster-a-positive-code-review-culture" class="text-xl font-semibold text-navy-600 mt-8 mb-3">8. Foster a positive code review culture</h3> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">Peer review can put strain on interpersonal team relationships. It&apos;s difficult to have every piece of work critiqued by peers and to have management evaluating and measuring defect density in your code. Therefore, in order for peer code review to be successful, it&apos;s extremely important that managers create a culture of collaboration and learning in peer review.</p> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">While it&apos;s easy to see defects as purely negative, each bug is actually an opportunity for the team to improve code quality. Peer review also allows junior team members to learn from senior leaders and for even the most experienced programmers to break bad habits.</p> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">Defects found in peer review are <em>not</em> an acceptable rubric by which to evaluate team members. Reports pulled from peer code reviews should never be used in performance reports. If personal metrics become a basis for compensation or promotion, developers will become hostile toward the process and naturally focus on improving personal metrics rather than writing better overall code.</p> <h3 id="embrace-the-subconscious-implications-of-peer-review" class="text-xl font-semibold text-navy-600 mt-8 mb-3">9. Embrace the subconscious implications of peer review</h3> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">The knowledge that others will be examining their work naturally drives people to produce a better product. This &quot;Ego Effect&quot; naturally incentivizes developers to write cleaner code because their peers will certainly see it. The SmartBear study of Cisco Systems found that &quot;spot checking&quot; 20% to 33% of the code resulted in lower defect density with minimal time expenditure. If your code has a 1-in-3 chance of being called out for review, that&apos;s enough of an incentive to double-check your work.</p> <h3 id="practice-lightweight-code-reviews" class="text-xl font-semibold text-navy-600 mt-8 mb-3">10. Practice lightweight code reviews</h3> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">Between email, over-the-shoulder, Microsoft Word, tool-assisted and hybrids of all types there are countless ways to collaboratively review code. However, to fully optimize your team&apos;s time and to effectively measure its results, a lightweight, tool-assisted process is recommended.</p> <p class="text-dark leading-relaxed mb-5">The SmartBear study of Cisco Systems found that <strong class="font-semibold">lightweight code review takes less than 20% the time of formal reviews and finds just as many bugs!</strong> Formal, or heavyweight, inspection averages nine hours per 200 LOC.
